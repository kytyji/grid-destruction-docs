    Effects and sounds¶

    Walls fire their own effects and sounds, where material actually came off, on every client. The weapon does not fire them at the bullet's point, a shot that opens nothing spawns nothing, and a player who joins mid-fight does not see old damage replayed.

    It works out of the box. The shipped DA_GridSurfaceMaterials has Niagara systems and sounds assigned for every material, and every GridDestructibleWall adds a GridDestructionFXComponent at runtime that plays them. There is nothing to wire. Nothing here replicates, and nothing plays on a dedicated server.

    Keep your weapon's impact effect

    It is instant for the shooter. The wall's effects arrive with replication, one network update later on a client. The weapon says "hit"; the wall says "this is what broke".

    The slots¶

    Each row of the surface material asset (Configs → a material → Effects) has four effect slots and three sound slots.

    slot fires once per +X points User parameters
    BreakFX material opens: a hole, an exit blowout, a cut through an end or a bore cluster of fine cells that opened together out of the face the material broke out through: the back for a shot from the front, the side itself for a cut through a cap or jamb CellCount, ExtentCm, Normal, ParticleBudget, ExitsBack, ExitsSide
    CollapseFX a piece of wall breaks away falling piece out of the wall's front SizeCm, CellCount, Normal
    LandingFX a falling piece hits something hard piece, once up SizeCm, CellCount, ImpactSpeed
    ImpactDustFX a hit knocks material off a face or a side without opening anything patch; at most MaxDustPerBatch per batch out of the face that lost it CellCount, RemovedDepthCm, ExtentCm, Normal
    sound slot plays notes
    BreakSound with BreakFX a short crunch with variations
    CollapseSound with CollapseFX; when set, BreakSound does not also play for those cells a heavy crack
    LandingSound with LandingFX the rubble must simulate physics
    • Fine resolution. Breaks and dust are detected per fine cell, so a bullet hole smaller than a cell fires them.
    • Drawn once. Cells opened by a collapse fire BreakFX only if CollapseFX is empty. A hole fires BreakFX, not dust as well. A corner shared by two joined walls fires on one wall only.
    • Niagara or Cascade. A slot accepts either.

    Parameters a system receives¶

    The system spawns rotated: local +X points as in the table above, and +Z up the wall (if +X itself points up or down, +Z points out of the wall's face). Author everything that should fly "out" along local +X, and everything that lies along the wall in local Y/Z. The effect then turns to fit any wall, including a sloped one.

    Declare the parameters in the system's User Parameters, with exactly these names and types. A parameter the wall does not set keeps its default, and a typo means the parameter always stays at its default.

    parameter type meaning
    User.ExtentCm float half the size of the break or patch: about 5–15 cm for a bullet, 50–150 cm for a grenade. The main scale parameter
    User.ParticleBudget int already scaled by sg.EffectsQuality (Low 3 / Medium 8 / High 15) and never above CellCount. Use it as the number of chunks
    User.CellCount int how many fine cells opened: 1–4 for a bullet at subdivision 3, tens to hundreds for a grenade
    User.Normal vector the wall's outward direction
    User.ExitsBack, User.ExitsSide bool the break came out of the back, or out of a side
    User.SizeCm float size of a falling piece
    User.ImpactSpeed float landing speed, usually 250–1500 cm/s
    User.RemovedDepthCm float how much material a dent removed in total: 1–5 cm for one bullet

    Tuning on the wall¶

    knob on default what it does
    MaxSpawnDistanceCm GridDestructionFX 6000 skips effects far from the local camera
    MinSoundIntervalSeconds GridDestructionFX 0.08 shortest gap between two sounds of one kind from one wall, so automatic fire does not become a wall of noise
    SoundVolumeScale GridDestructionFX 1 the wall's overall volume
    bEmitDust GridDebrisSpawner on turns the dent dust off
    MaxDustPerBatch GridDebrisSpawner 4 caps dust per batch
    DustMinErosionBytes GridDebrisSpawner 3 ignores the trickle the far-face coupling adds around a hit
    LandingMinSpeedCmS GridCollapseVisual 250 how fast a piece must be moving for a sudden stop to count as a landing

    Per-wall override. In a wall Blueprint, add GridDestructionFXComponent yourself, tick bOverrideMaterialEffects and fill OverrideEffects. The wall does not add a second one.

    The shipped sounds share SCon_GridDestruction (a Sound Concurrency) and SA_GridDestruction (attenuation) in /GridDestruction/Audio/. A grenade opens many clusters at once, so keep a concurrency limit on any sounds you assign.

    Making your own systems¶

    The shipped systems are a starting point. When you replace them, these are the rules that make wall effects read as physical.

    Three layers¶

    Real destruction is three things at once, and a fake-looking effect almost always shows only one:

    layer what it is how it behaves
    chunks pieces of 1–8 cm leave fast, fall on a parabola, bounce once and lie still
    grit sand, under 1 cm fastest, short-lived, barely visible one by one
    dust a cloud starts fast, brakes hard, grows 2–4×, settles and fades over 2–4 s
    • Dust is the wall's colour, lighter and greyer. Brick dusts reddish, concrete grey; wood gives splinters and sawdust rather than dust.
    • Timing. Chunks and grit are done in the first 0.1 s; dust lives 20–40 times longer.
    • Size follows the hole. Scale by ExtentCm. A bullet hole with a cloud the size of half the wall reads worse than no effect.
    • Lit particles. Unlit dust glows in shadow. Use a lit translucent material (Volumetric PerVertex NonDirectional is cheap) with Depth Fade 20–50 cm.

    Chunks (GPU)¶

    1. Sim target GPU, local space off, fixed bounds on (about ±300 cm).
    2. Spawn burst: count = User.ParticleBudget.
    3. Spawn position: a box (2, ExtentCm × 2, ExtentCm × 2) in local space, not a point: the YZ plane is the wall.
    4. Velocity: a cone around local +X, 35–55°, 150–600 cm/s. Smaller pieces faster. Optionally 20–30% at half speed along −X: the entry side loses grit too.
    5. Gravity and drag 0.4–1.2: without drag every piece flies the same arc.
    6. Mesh renderer with 4–6 chunk meshes, random index and rotation, sizes weighted to the small end.
    7. GPU depth-buffer collision, restitution 0.05–0.2, high friction.
    8. Freeze on first contact. When the collision flag is set, zero the velocity, gravity and drag, and the rotation rate (or scale it by saturate(length(Velocity) / 50)), or a landed chip spins until its lifetime ends. A settled particle then costs rendering only, and depth-buffer collision, which only knows what is on screen, no longer matters to it.
    9. Scale the mesh to 0 over the last 10% of its life.

    Dust¶

    CPU is fine (few particles). 4–12 sprites from a disc of radius ExtentCm × 0.6 in the YZ plane; velocity along +X 100–300 cm/s in a 30–45° cone with drag 3–6, so the cloud bursts out and hangs; size grows ×2–4 over its life; alpha peaks at 0.25–0.5 quickly and fades slowly; life 1.5–4 s; slow rotation and a weak curl noise.

    Recipes per slot¶

    • BreakFX = chunks + grit + dust. Multiply count and speed by ExitsBack ? 1.3 : 1.0 for a bigger exit than entry. For a bullet keep it short and modest (3–8 chunks, 4–6 puffs, gone in 2 s); a grenade is the same system scaled by its parameters. Check both.
    • ImpactDustFX is the most frequent effect, so keep it the cheapest: 3–6 puffs, alpha ≤ 0.3, life 1–2 s, scaled by clamp(RemovedDepthCm / 4, 0.4, 1.5).
    • CollapseFX: large, slow dust from the piece's volume (30–120 cm/s, grows ×3, 3–5 s, settles down) and 10–30 small pieces falling from the break with almost no initial speed.
    • LandingFX: +X is up, so the YZ plane is the floor. A ring of dust of radius SizeCm × 0.5, moving radially outward at 80–250 cm/s with drag 3–5, strength clamp((ImpactSpeed − 250) / 1000, 0.3, 1).

    Performance rules¶

    The component takes systems from Niagara's pool and culls them by distance. For that to work:

    1. Every system must finish. All emitters Loop Behavior Once, the system's Inactive Response Complete or Kill. An endless emitter never returns to the pool: a leak you only see after an hour of play.
    2. Give the systems an Effect Type with a max distance around 6000 cm, an instance cap of 16–32 per system, and scalability by sg.EffectsQuality. Its visibility culling must Deactivate, not Pause: a paused system off screen keeps its particles forever.
    3. GPU emitters need fixed bounds.
    4. No per-pixel sorting, distortion or lights on dust. Light renderers only for metal sparks, only on High.
    5. Check: stat Niagara after a minute of shooting should level off, not climb. fx.DumpNCPoolInfo shows pooled components in use.

    Checklist per material¶

    • dust matches the wall's colour, but lighter
    • dust brakes and grows rather than flying evenly
    • chunks vary in size, small ones fly further
    • one system gives a small bullet effect and a large grenade effect
    • a shot through from the front throws chunks out of the back
    • a shot into an end or a reveal dusts out of that side
    • particles are lit (check in shadow)
    • the system finishes (pool instances do not grow)
    • it looks decent on Low

    Driving your own effects from events¶

    To spawn effects yourself instead of through the slots, leave the slots empty and bind the wall's events. GridDebrisSpawnerComponent spawns nothing itself: it watches cells open, groups them into clusters, applies a budget, primes a baseline so a joining client does not replay every old hole, and broadcasts.

    event on fires
    OnDebrisBurstBP GridDebrisSpawner once per cluster of opened cells, with FGridDebrisBurst
    OnDustBP GridDebrisSpawner a dent that opened nothing
    OnChunkDetachedBP GridCollapseVisual a piece breaks away
    OnChunkLandedBP GridCollapseVisual a falling piece lands

    The collapse events fire after the component has finished its own work, so a listener may damage the wall safely.

    FGridDebrisBurst:

    field use it for
    WorldLocation where to spawn: the cluster centre, on the wall's mid-surface
    WorldNormal the wall's outward direction, always the front
    ExitNormal, bExitsBack, bExitsSide which way to throw: out of the face the material broke out through
    CellCount how many cells opened, counted at the finest level. Scale the burst; never spawn per cell
    ExtentCm half-size of the cluster: how wide to spread the spawn
    Surface which system and material to pick
    bFromCollapse the cells were opened by a collapse
    SuggestedParticleCount already scaled by sg.EffectsQuality

    One system per surface type beats one system with a switch: the mesh, material and size distribution all differ.

    What this deliberately does not do¶

    • Particles are never shootable and never block anything. Settled geometry that blocked shots would stand in a different place on every machine, because rubble does not replicate. Nothing in gameplay depends on where particles land.
    • A doorway-sized plank is not a particle. Particles work at fist scale and below. A large falling piece is real geometry for about a second, from GridCollapseVisualComponent; particles carry what is left on the floor.
    • Two clients may disagree about the dust. That is fine, and it costs no bandwidth.
