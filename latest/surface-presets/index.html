    Surface presets¶

    Every number a wall needs to fail like the thing it is made of, for all six surface types, with what each one changes and the order to tune them in.

    Three assets carry them, and the split matters:

    asset answers
    GridSurfaceMaterialDataAsset (shipped: DA_GridSurfaceMaterials) how this material fails: shared by every wall made of it
    GridWallPreset (shipped: GP_*) what this wall is: thickness, density, how much of it may go
    GridCollapseVisualComponent on the wall what its rubble does after it falls

    Use one project-wide surface material asset and reference it from every preset. A surface type missing from its Configs map falls back to neutral values, so a partial map is valid.

    Editing the numbers: GridMaterials.csv¶

    Every number in tables 1 and 2 lives in one spreadsheet, Config/GridMaterials.csv in the plugin's folder: one column per preset, one row per field, and a Notes column saying what each does. The plugin's folder is Plugins/GridDestruction/ in your project, or Engine/Plugins/Marketplace/GridDestruction/ for a Fab install. Edit the CSV in Excel, Google Sheets or a text editor, then in the editor choose Tools → Execute Python Script and pick scripts/ue_apply_material_table.py from the same folder. The Python Editor Script Plugin must be enabled (it is by default).

    It creates or updates each column's preset (row Asset) and its entry in the surface material asset (DA.* rows), then presses Apply Preset on every wall in the open level that uses one of them. Save the level afterwards. The log prints each field it changed as old -> new.

    • An empty cell leaves that value alone.
    • Point Asset at your own preset to tune it from the table; add a column for a second kind of brick. Two columns of one SurfaceType must agree on the DA.* rows, and the script stops if they do not.
    • WallMesh and WallMaterial take asset paths. A preset the script created has neither, so fill them before a wall uses it.
    • Masks, effects and CapEdges stay on the assets.
    • The CSV wins. A value you changed by hand in the editor is overwritten on the next run unless its cell is empty.
    • It reaches only walls in the open level whose preset is a column. In another level using the same presets, run it there too or press Apply Preset; a wall that missed it logs GRIDSTAMP at BeginPlay.

    Work on copies

    Copy the shipped GP_* presets and the surface material asset into your project, and point the CSV's Asset and SurfaceMaterialData rows at the copies. A plugin update then never overwrites your tuning.

    Why each column looks the way it does¶

    • Concrete is the reference: resistance 1.0, chaotic spall on both axes, a circular blast. Every other column is a departure from it.
    • Brick fails along its courses. QuantiseU 0.15 against AmplitudeU 0.30 gives discrete levels that read as mortar joints rather than noise, and AmplitudeV 0.04 keeps the courses straight. FalloffScaleU 1.6 against 0.7 spreads a hit sideways along a course instead of down through one.
    • Stone is the hardest masonry: high resistance, the highest DepthNoise so the crater surface is rough, and a low smoothing angle so chips stay faceted.
    • Wood splits along the grain: FalloffScaleU 3.0 against 1.0 makes a hit reach three times as far along the grain as across it. U and V are the visible texture's axes, so rotating Wall | UV by 90° for upright boards turns the grain with them.
    • Metal deforms rather than breaks: the highest resistance, the smallest edge amplitude and a 60° smoothing angle, so it reads as bent sheet.
    • Glass shatters: the lowest resistance, the largest amplitude and a hard 15° smoothing angle.

    The fields¶

    DamageResistance
    How much of each bite this material takes. It changes how many shots a wall takes and almost nothing about how it looks. Thickness is the real durability knob; resistance saturates once a blast is deep enough to open a cell.
    FalloffScaleU / FalloffScaleV
    The blast's shape along and across the texture. The single most recognisable knob, and it costs nothing.
    SmoothingAngleDeg
    Normals are smoothed across folds flatter than this. -1 means unset (the wall mesh component's own value), 0 is flat shading. An angle above atan(halfThickness / cellSize) never engages: at 10 cm cells and 15 cm thickness that limit is 37°.
    RicochetAngleDeg / GrazingDamageScale
    What a shallow hit takes off. The angle is measured off the surface: 90 is straight on, 0 is along the wall. Below it the bite ramps down to GrazingDamageScale at 0°. A ricochet angle of 0 means the material never grazes. Keep GrazingDamageScale above 0 for anything that spalls: a skipping round still ploughs a furrow, and a shot that leaves no mark reads as a broken wall. The plugin decides how much material came off; whether the projectile deflects, sparks or stops is your weapon's call, using Obliquity and bGrazing in the damage result.
    EdgeProfile.Style
    How a hole's outline is drawn. Square follows the cells' sides, honest for brick. Shard cuts outward corners on the diagonal, so a staircase becomes a straight cut. Rounded relaxes the contour so stairs become straight runs and corners round off. Render only: the collider stays on the cells.
    EdgeProfile.AmplitudeU/V, QuantiseU/V, DepthNoise
    The rim's jitter in the wall's plane, its banding, and the roughness of the crater's floor. Amplitude above 0.40 is refused: two neighbouring vertices would swap places. Keep Quantise around half the amplitude; at twice the amplitude or more every vertex rounds to zero and the axis has no jitter at all. Apply Preset warns about both.
    bBrittle, BrittleBreakFraction, BrittleShardSizeCm
    A brittle cell is whole or gone, so a pane never shows a dish or a dent. BrittleBreakFraction is the share of a cell's thickness one hit must take to break it for certain; smaller hits break it with a proportional chance, so a blast's edge cracks unevenly and repeated hits always get through. Lower it for thin panes, raise it for laminated glass. The pane is divided into irregular shards about BrittleShardSizeCm across, and a hit breaks out whole shards connected to the impact. The roll is made on the server, so every machine sees the same holes.

    Two kinds of brick¶

    SurfaceType has six values and no Custom. That is deliberate: the enum is a lookup key for "what is this physically", for effects and sounds. Everything that differs between a red brick and a grey one lives on the preset.

    You want Do this
    Two brick walls that break differently Two presets, both SurfaceType = Brick
    ...that also resist differently Point their SurfaceMaterialData at two different data assets
    Glass that shatters vs glass that spiders Two presets, different FractureWeightMask

    Size and structure¶

    ThicknessCm, CellDensity
    How deep the wall is, and cells per metre. Bigger cells hold more depth, so a wall that opens with no dent first wants a lower density or more thickness.
    SubdivisionFactor
    A cell splits into factor × factor fine children on its first bite. Detail, and its cost on the wire, exist only where the wall was hit. Raise it when a MASKSCALE line asks for it, or when an edge on show looks too smooth.
    StructuralThreshold
    The remaining fraction below which a cell stops carrying load. Wood gives up its load path long before it opens; reinforced concrete carries down to a shell; glass sheds whole panes at the first serious crack.
    MaxDestructionPercent
    The share of the wall that shooting may remove: 0 for a level boundary, 0.3 for a load-bearing element. Per wall, not per material, which is why the row is uniform. It gates shooting only: a severed piece still falls, and a collapse counts against the cap.

    MaxOverhangCm and BuiltOverhangs¶

    A cell is held only if it is within MaxOverhangCm sideways of a column that stands on, or hangs from, a support. Up and down are free on an upright wall, so an intact wall always stands; a cantilever longer than the limit loses its tip, and a beam held at both ends spans twice the limit. On a ceiling or floor neither axis is free.

    • Keep it above half the widest opening with no wall under it, or the lintel over it falls on the first hit anywhere on the wall.
    • 0 turns it off. The limit is rounded down to whole cells.

    What was built stands: the limit counts from how far a wall reached when damage first came to it, so it drops only what damage leaves overhanging. A wall built past the limit logs an OVERHANG line once. BuiltOverhangs on the preset (Wall | Durability, advanced) picks a stricter rule:

    value a span built past the limit
    Keep (default) stands; the limit judges only what damage leaves
    SettleSilently is gone when the level starts, with no debris or sound
    Collapse comes down when the level starts, with debris and falling pieces

    Shape of a hit¶

    MinSpallRadiusCells
    Every hit is spread over at least this radius. If an edge looks too smooth, lower it to 0.8–1.0 on brick and concrete for a more local bite, and raise FractureNoise to keep small holes from reading as squares. Do not take it to 0.
    ExitSpallDepthScale, ExitSpallRadiusScale
    The blowout of the far face, as a fraction of the wall's thickness and a multiple of the hit's radius. It starts once the deepest cell in a hit is eaten past ThroughCouplingThreshold of the thickness (0.8 by default, on the wall's GridData component). Brittle materials throw more off the back; metal petals instead. 0 switches it off for the material.
    ObliquityStretch, ObliquitySkew
    A glancing hit becomes a furrow instead of a dish. The stretch lengthens the crater along the shot's direction; the skew slides it forward. Keep the two equal: that pins the trailing edge where a square-on crater's would be and sends all the added length forward. Below it the crater grows backwards, above it the crater leaves the impact point.
    FractureNoise, FractureNoiseScaleCm
    Varies each cell's resistance by a fixed amount, so a small hole does not read as a square. Above about 0.6 the rim turns noisy and specks survive inside holes. Clamped at 0.9.
    FractureMaskContrast, bDeriveFractureMaskFromMaterial
    Strength of the break pattern, and whether it comes from the wall material's height map. See Setup → Where the wall breaks.
    SpikeSuppressionChance, MinSupportingNeighbours
    Clean slivers off a hole's rim. A cell with fewer standing neighbours than the minimum is pruned with this chance. The chance is 0.7 on purpose: a rim with every spike pruned reads as machined. Keep the minimum at 2: higher strips the rim of every hole. By default the chance follows the break pattern, so brick cleans up along its joints and keeps its teeth on the bricks. SpikeSuppressionMask paints it per wall; SpikeMaskStrength sets how far a mask moves it.

    Edges and rims¶

    HoleRimThicknessFraction
    The band of wall left standing at a hole's edge, as a fraction of the thickness: what makes a hole read as a break in a solid rather than a cut in paper. Thin materials need more to show anything. It is also depth the crater cannot reach, so too much flattens craters; above about 0.5 a hole reads as a bevelled port.
    EdgeCrumbleScale
    How much an exposed edge (a capped side, a reveal) wanders as it erodes: crisp when intact, the material's own jitter when eaten. It needs a real EdgeProfile amplitude to show.
    RevealCrushScale
    A shot into a side (a reveal, a capped end, the bore of a hole) cuts that side along its own normal by PeakDepthCm × RevealCrushScale. 0 makes such a shot an ordinary face hit.
    CornerChipScale
    A shot at the face near an edge also chips the edge. Values near 1 make an edge give way about as fast as a shot straight into it; the shipped 4 makes corners crumble visibly faster than the face. Lower it toward 1–1.5 for a sturdier look. 0 switches it off.
    EdgeFirst
    In a cell at an edge, a face hit dents the face by 1 − EdgeFirst of its bite. At 1 the corner crumbles away before the face behind it gives. Does nothing while CornerChipScale is 0.
    DentStepCm
    Dents are drawn in whole steps of this depth, so a hit drops a chip instead of crumpling the face. A hit shallower than one step leaves the geometry flat and is shown by the damage material. The collider uses the same steps. 0 draws the exact depth.
    BreachFalloffCells
    Purely visual: how far, in cells, the "near a hole" channel of the damage material reaches. Glass at 4.0 is the deliberate outlier: its cracks radiate far past what was removed.

    CapEdges: closing the wall's own sides¶

    Per placement, not per material. A wall's outer perimeter is open by default, because most walls are seated in a frame or a facade. Tick the sides a player can see:

    the wall is tick
    seated in a frame, or flush between two others nothing
    protruding from another wall at 90° the exposed end only
    a free-standing barricade or a pillar of wall all four
    a panel whose top the player can see over Top

    A cap breaks with the wall: it is built from the same cells as a hole's rim. If a capped edge looks like a knife edge, raise HoleRimThicknessFraction.

    Checking that a wall runs what the preset says¶

    GridDestruction.CheckPresets

    GRIDSTAMP [BP_Wall_Brick_7]: MATCH against preset 'GP_Brick' — ...
GRIDSTAMP [BP_Wall_Brick_9]: STALE against preset 'GP_Brick' — 1 field(s) differ:
    MinSupportingNeighbours live=2 preset=3. ...
GRIDSTAMP SUMMARY: 11 wall(s) — 1 stale, 0 without a preset, 10 current.

    STALE means your edit never reached that wall: press Apply Preset on it and save the level. MATCH means the wall really runs the asset's values, so a knob that still looks inert is not a stamping problem. The same check runs on its own at BeginPlay on the server, and Log Preset Stamp Drift beside Apply Preset checks one wall. It reports and never corrects: a value set on the component alone is a legitimate per-wall override.

    A Blueprint's own Class Defaults override the C++ defaults. If a knob does nothing, look at the placed actor's Details panel before anywhere else.

    Tuning order¶

    The tables are meant to be right on the first build. When a wall does not look like its material, work in this order, because each step changes what the next one is looking at. Change one number at a time.

    1. Silhouette: does the hole have the right shape?¶

    Look at one hole from the front.

    what you see do
    the hole is a square raise FractureNoise, or give it a break pattern
    the rim is noisy rather than rough lower FractureNoise below 0.6
    specks of wall survive inside the hole lower FractureNoise, or raise SpikeSuppressionChance
    the rim has no teeth raise EdgeProfile.AmplitudeU/V, or lower SpikeSuppressionChance
    brick cracks through the bricks, not the joints give it the material's height map, or raise FractureMaskContrast

    2. Thickness: does the hole read as a break in a solid?¶

    Look at the same hole from an angle.

    what you see do
    the edge is a knife edge raise HoleRimThicknessFraction
    a visible band, but the crater looks shallow lower it
    the wall's own outer edge is open tick the right CapEdges
    a shot at a doorway's reveal dents the face raise RevealCrushScale
    a shot at the face next to an edge never chips it raise CornerChipScale
    the rim looks like the face, not like broken material use the damage material

    3. Depth: does a hit dent before it opens?¶

    Shoot the same spot repeatedly.

    what you see do
    the wall opens with no dent first lower CellDensity, or raise ThicknessCm
    the crater is a flat disc raise EdgeProfile.DepthNoise
    the crater bottoms out early lower HoleRimThicknessFraction
    the far face stays flat until it opens lower ThroughCouplingThreshold on the wall, or raise ExitSpallDepthScale
    holes are crisp but cost too much lower SubdivisionFactor; see Multiplayer

    4. Identity: is it the right material?¶

    The three knobs that carry most of a material's identity, in order: FalloffScaleU/V, SmoothingAngleDeg, EdgeProfile.QuantiseU. DamageResistance comes last: it changes how many shots a wall takes, not how it looks.

    Then fire along the wall:

    what you see do
    a glancing hit leaves the same disc as a square one ObliquityStretch is 0: type table 2's row
    the furrow reaches behind where the round touched raise ObliquitySkew to equal ObliquityStretch
    the crater has left the impact point lower ObliquitySkew below ObliquityStretch
    a glancing hit on steel digs as deep as a square one RicochetAngleDeg is 0: type table 1's row

    5. Weight: does the rubble belong to the wall?¶

    Author PhysicalSurface physical materials before touching DebrisMassScale.
